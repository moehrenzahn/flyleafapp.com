Hi, I’m Max! I’ve been reading MacStories long enough to remember when the “Mac” in the title was still the main topic of the site. My read-later archive can prove it.

I’ve seen many read-later apps come and go, but I never found one that cared about design, typography, and the reading experience as much as I do – especially on the Mac. So I built it myself: **[Flyleaf](https://flyleafapp.com) is the read-later app for those who care about reading, not just collecting articles.**

![](/images/illustration/Flyleaf_Hero_1456x850.png)

Flyleaf saves any web page you throw at it and turns it into something you’ll actually want to read: without popups, ads, trackers, or anything else that gets between you and the content.

I believe vertical scrolling text is not great for focused reading. That’s why Flyleaf lets you read page by page, like a magazine. While other apps include this as an afterthought at best, it’s at the core of the design of Flyleaf.

I built Flyleaf to the standards I myself expect from great apps. It follows Apple platform conventions on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. It respects your time and attention. It gives you control over your data, including deep Shortcuts support. It’s hand-crafted, not vibe-coded.

Give Flyleaf a try with the next MacStories story you read! [It’s a free download on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/flyleaf-read-later/id6475200381), with an optional subscription. I think it’s pretty neat. Maybe you will, too.
